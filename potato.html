    Why grow potatoes?

    Potatoes are a versatile food crop that can support household food supplies and create income. A healthy crop starts with suitable conditions, good-quality seed and regular care.

    Choose a good site and prepare the soil

    Choose a sunny site with loose, fertile, well-drained soil. Potatoes need steady moisture, but waterlogged soil can cause tubers to rot. Remove weeds and stones, then mix in well-rotted compost or manure before planting.

    Select seed and plant

    Start with healthy, certified seed potatoes suited to your area; saved or diseased tubers can carry problems into a new crop. Plant with the shoots facing up and cover with soil. Follow local agricultural advice for planting dates, depth and spacing, since these vary with variety and growing conditions.

    Care for the growing crop

    Water regularly when rainfall is limited, aiming to keep the soil evenly moist rather than saturated. Remove weeds while plants are young. As stems grow, pull loose soil around their bases (“earth up”) to cover developing tubers and help protect them from sunlight, which can turn potatoes green.

    Watch for pests and disease

    Check leaves and stems often for unusual spots, wilting, insect damage or weak growth. Remove volunteer potato plants and rotate crops so potatoes are not repeatedly grown in the same bed. If disease spreads quickly, seek advice from a local agricultural extension officer before choosing a treatment.

    Harvest and store safely

    When the plants yellow and die back, check that tubers have reached a useful size before harvesting. Lift them gently to avoid cuts and bruises, then let the skins dry in a shaded, well-ventilated place. Store sound potatoes in a cool, dry and dark place, and use damaged tubers first.

    Keep learning locally

    Weather, soil and suitable varieties differ across Zambia. For locally recommended planting times, varieties and crop protection, consult your district agricultural extension officer or a trusted seed supplier.
